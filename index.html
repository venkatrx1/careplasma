The Basics

What is plasma, exactly?

Plasma is the straw-colored liquid that makes up over half of your blood. It carries red and white blood cells and platelets throughout your body, and it's rich in proteins — antibodies, clotting factors, and albumin — that can't be manufactured synthetically.

Donated plasma is manufactured into therapies for people with bleeding disorders, primary immune deficiencies, burns, and trauma. Donating is simple: a machine draws your blood, separates the plasma, and safely returns your red cells and platelets to you.

Six Steps, One Hour

The plasma donation process

Every visit follows the same careful sequence, from eligibility check to fluid replacement — designed for your safety and comfort at every stage.

01

Check eligibility

Confirm you meet age, weight, and health requirements before booking your first visit.

02

Make an appointment

Call ahead or simply walk in during our hours of operation — no long wait for a first slot.

03

Register

Complete a short form with your personal and medical history, and present valid ID, proof of address, and proof of Social Security.

04

Health screening

A medical professional checks your weight, blood pressure, and hemoglobin, and reviews your health history and medications.

05

Plasma collection

A needle draws blood that's separated into plasma by a specialized machine; your remaining blood components are returned to you. About 45–60 minutes.

06

Fluid replacement

Saline restores your fluid balance, and our team confirms your next eligible donation date before you head out.

Frequently Asked

Questions, answered

Can't find what you're looking for? Email info@careplasma.com.

What's the difference between donating plasma and donating blood?

Plasma donation collects only the liquid component of your blood and takes longer than whole-blood donation because the plasma is separated using a special machine — which is why donors are compensated for their time. Plasma can be donated up to twice in a 7-day period, while whole blood is limited to once every 56 days, since the body replenishes plasma faster than red blood cells.

How long does the process take?

Typically 60–90 minutes from check-in to completion, though timing can vary by visit and donor.

How often can I donate?

Most donors can give plasma up to twice per week, subject to our guidelines and your individual health status.

How much will I get paid?

Compensation varies with current promotions — new donors can earn up to $500 in their first month.

Does it hurt?

The needle insertion causes brief, mild discomfort, but the donation itself shouldn't be painful. Some donors experience mild dizziness or fatigue that typically resolves quickly.

Can I donate if I've received a COVID-19 vaccine?

Yes — most vaccinated donors are eligible, though waiting periods can vary by vaccine type and current policy. Our staff can confirm at check-in.

How is donated plasma used?

It's manufactured into medications for immune disorders and clotting factor deficiencies, and used in treating burns and trauma injuries, as well as in developing new therapies.
