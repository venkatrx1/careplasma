FAQs

Frequently Asked

Questions, answered

Please reach us at info@careplasma.com if you can't find an answer to your question.

What's the difference between donating plasma and donating blood?

Plasma donation collects only the liquid component of your blood and takes longer than whole-blood donation because the plasma is separated using a special machine — which is why donors are compensated for their time. Plasma can be donated up to twice in a 7-day period, while whole blood is limited to once every 56 days, since the body replenishes plasma faster than red blood cells.

How long does the process take?

Typically 60–90 minutes from check-in to completion, though timing can vary by visit and donor.

How often can I donate?

Most donors can give plasma up to twice per week, subject to our guidelines and your individual health status.

How much will I get paid?

Compensation varies with current promotions — new donors can earn up to $500 in their first month.

Does it hurt?

The needle insertion causes brief, mild discomfort, but the donation itself shouldn't be painful. Some donors experience mild dizziness or fatigue that typically resolves quickly.

Can I donate if I've received a COVID-19 vaccine?

Yes — most vaccinated donors are eligible, though waiting periods can vary by vaccine type and current policy. Our staff can confirm at check-in.

How is donated plasma used?

It's manufactured into medications for immune disorders and clotting factor deficiencies, and used in treating burns and trauma injuries, as well as in developing new therapies.
